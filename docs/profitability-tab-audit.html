01

The verdict, in one paragraph

The plumbing is sound; the inputs are half-filled. The model's arithmetic is trustworthy — the eleven cost buckets sum to the view's stated margin to the cent, no dollar lands in two buckets, and 114 unit tests pass. Every bucket that has data behind it is wired correctly and labelled with its own provenance.

What is not trustworthy is the completeness of what feeds it. Four fifths of COGS is a roadmap projection rather than an invoiced build cost. The entire acquisition half of the tab — CAC, LTV:CAC, payback, lifetime profit — is inert, because all $69,134 of Meta spend carries a single date stamp that predates every customer it won. Three cost buckets have never been priced. Roughly $22,000 of real cost is missing.

Read the headline −$14,443 as directionally right and precisely wrong: the business is losing money per customer, the model is honest about saying so, but the true figure could move several thousand dollars in either direction once the items in section 5 are closed.

Since the first draft: cancelled orders now keep their revenue and lose their cost (V16, shipped today — see A3). The refund reverses the sale once, as it always did, and the $9,503.66 of machines LILA kept moved to a company-level line outside the customer model. Margin improved by $15,556.52. One new finding came out of it, A0, and it is the mirror image of the problem the change was meant to fix.

04

What is live and accurate

Quotable today, with the caveat attached to each.

  • Revenue, gross revenue and discounts. $202,099.47 net of tax across 233 matched sale orders, with $16,263.85 of discount visible separately — a 7.4% average giveaway. The founder-cohort invoices are booked (total less HST, deposits included). One sale order worth $10.42 matches no customer.
  • Freight, for the Freightcom era. All 158 shipments now carry invoiced charges (169 charge rows), and the coalesce is per shipment, so multi-leg orders no longer lose their un-invoiced legs. Average $124.27 a shipment.
  • Warranty cost. Replacement orders costed at COGS + freight, with cancelled replacements correctly excluded. 42 customers have had one.
  • Support labour. All 42 diagnosis calls carry a duration; none fall back to "unpriced". The $75/person-hour blended rate is the only assumption in the bucket.
  • Consumables. 78 retail receipts, actual amounts, correctly filed as cost of goods rather than freight.
  • Geography and cohorts. Of 206 active customers, only 9 lack a region and 7 a country — the ship-to on the first sale order beats the CRM record, which is the right precedence. 21 have no cohort date.
  • The provenance labelling itself. Every bucket renders its own basis chip, the customer card lists its specific gaps, and the "what these numbers do and don't cover" panel names the unpriced buckets and the unallocated spend. The tab does not pretend.
  • The arithmetic. 114 tests pass across the model and the tab, including a powers-of-two check that would catch any duplicated bucket.

A2

CAC, LTV:CAC and payback are all inert

All 40 rows in fb_campaigns share one date_start of 2023-07-28, so the monthly rollup lands the entire $69,133.81 of Meta spend in July 2023. Every paid-social customer was acquired between December 2025 and June 2026. The two sets never intersect: 100% of spend goes unallocated, every customer's CAC is $0 on a basis of "no spend on file", LTV:CAC is blank, payback reads "0 at sale", and lifetime profit is contribution margin under another name.

acquisition_spend_manual is empty, so the 100 non-team customers who arrived through organic search (47), organic social (28), referral (22) and email (3) have no spend feed at all.

Send Monthly Meta spend for Dec 2025 → Aug 2026, or a fix to the sync so it pulls monthly insights rather than lifetime totals. Plus any spend behind the other four channels — SEO retainer, referral incentives, email tooling.

A3

Shipped 28 August as customer_profitability V16. When an order is cancelled LILA keeps the machine, so its COGS and freight no longer sit on the person who cancelled — charging them for a machine that came back made a cancellation read as the worst sale in the book.

09

How this was verified

Every figure above was read from the live database on 28 August 2026, not from the model's own summaries. The audit ran three ways:

  • Against the view. All eleven buckets summed independently and checked against net_margin_cad — they agree to the cent, before and after V16. Each component was checked in isolation too: COGS −$14,777.76, freight −$778.68, revenue and order count unchanged at $202,099.47 and 233, margin +$15,556.52, and the five refunded cancellations re-checked one by one — two of them land on exactly $0.00, which is the test that the reversal is counted once.
  • Around the view. Each bucket's source table queried directly to find records the view's joins miss: refunded orders with no approval, shipped orders with no shipment, returns with no freight, shipments with no order, machines with no order.
  • Against the code. lib/profitability.ts, ProfitabilityTab.tsx and the view definition read line by line for definition mismatches — which is where B3, B5 and B6 came from. 114 tests pass.

Shipped during this audit: V15 (20260828120000_customer_profitability_v15_cancelled_orders.sql, 20260828130000_retained_units_team_split.sql) and its correction V16 (20260828140000_customer_profitability_v16_cancelled_cost_only.sql), all applied to the live database, with 1,445 tests and a clean production build behind them. V15 removed a cancelled order's revenue as well as its cost, which double-counted the refund; V16 keeps the revenue and moves only the cost.
