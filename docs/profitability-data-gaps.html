The founder-cohort invoices, and one thing PAYMENT doesn’t tell you

You asked me to pull revenue from the sales invoices, using the figure beside PAYMENT. I read all 17 PDFs on file for the 24 affected customers. The figure is there — but on 13 of the 14 sale invoices it is not the whole amount collected.

PAYMENT excludes the deposit

These invoices bill in two parts. Michael Madigan’s 1179 reads TOTAL 1,067.85, DEPOSIT 200.00, PAYMENT 867.85. He paid $1,067.85 — PAYMENT is only the balance after the deposit.

Across the 14 invoices, PAYMENT alone sums to $10,741.92; the amount actually collected is $13,541.92. Taking PAYMENT literally would understate revenue by $2,800 — every deposit ever taken. Only Lynn Liu’s 1192 has no deposit line, which is why PAYMENT there equals the total.

I’ve used TOTAL minus HST below. Tax is collected for the government, so the model excludes it from revenue — the same rule Shopify orders already follow.

What the numbers then showed

13 of the 15 sold below build cost. Against the $913.89 schedule COGS for that era, the 20–30% founder discounts put most of the cohort underwater before freight. Jim Christie lands at −$798, Sharon Corcoran −$776, Vicki Crooke −$736. Only Lynn Liu (+$125) and Mary & Marilynne Oskamp (+$234) clear — the two who took the smallest discount.

Read this as provisional. That COGS is the schedule projection converted at today’s FX, not an invoiced 2023/24 build cost — which is gap 3 below. If the real build cost then was lower, the cohort moves back toward break-even.
